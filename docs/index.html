One question. The same provision, in four languages.

EU law is enacted in 24 equally authentic language versions. Ask in English and this returns the provision that answers you — in Dutch, German or French — as verbatim EUR-Lex text with a link anchored to the article. Nothing is generated, so there is nothing to hallucinate.

Hybrid 0.222 and dense 0.219 differ by 0.003 — about 0.2 standard errors on 564 queries. That is not a win. Both clear BM25 by 5.5 standard errors, which is.

Before chunking, fusing lexical and dense retrieval bought a real 19% over the best single system, and that was the headline result here. It no longer holds. Once the embedder could read whole articles it matched the hybrid on its own, and fusion now costs 0.007 cross-lingually, because mixing a 0.095 system into a 0.190 one dilutes it. The hybrid had been compensating for truncation. Routing by whether the query and target language match — hybrid when they agree, dense when they do not — scores about 0.227, and is the honest design.

How often is it actually right

It narrows 1,195 articles to ten, and is right about half the time.

Measured over the 141 same-language questions. These are the numbers a compliance team would plan around, so they are stated plainly rather than folded into an average.

the cited article is the top result
50%

57%

This is a shortlisting tool, not an oracle. The five instruments run to 300 articles per language, 335 KB of statute for MiFID II alone; reading ten cited provisions takes a minute, and well over half the time the right one is among them. Note where chunking helped: rank-1 barely moved, but top-ten recall went from 45% to 57% — it pulls the right article into the shortlist rather than to the top of it. Every candidate arrives with the article number, the CELEX id, the version indexed and a deep link, so the reader confirms or discards it without leaving the page.

The bug that was costing the most

The retriever was reading a fraction of each article.

multilingual-e5-small has a 512-token window, and the corpus does not fit in it. 36 of the 63 cited articles — 57% — exceed it. MiFID II Article 4 is 4,955 tokens, 9.7× the window, so 90% of the most-cited provision in the corpus was invisible. The embedder was being scored on text it never saw.

Negative result, and a correction

Fine-tuning did not help — and the reason I gave was wrong.

Contrastive fine-tuning was tried twice and made retrieval worse both times, 0.154 to 0.146. Inverting the pair mix — capping cross-lingual alignment pairs, replacing formulaic templates with Inverse Cloze Task pairs — moved overall nDCG from 0.147 to 0.146. The obvious explanation was tested and rejected.

I then attributed the failure to corpus size: 690 trainable articles being too few for contrastive adaptation to beat general-purpose pretraining. That was a guess, and the measurement contradicts it.

Training ran at a 192-token window. 75% of articles are longer, and the median is 386 tokens — so the model learned half a provision, then was asked to retrieve a 512-token representation of it.

The positives it trained on and the passages it was scored against were different text. Both ends now match: pairs are built from the same 400-token chunks the index serves, and the training window equals the inference encoder's. That re-run is pending, and whatever it returns will be reported here — including if it fails again. The corpus-size claim was never measured; the truncation was.

Evaluation design

The relevance labels come from the regulator, not from us.

ESMA and Joint Committee Q&A documents state a question, an answer, and the articles the answer relies on. That yields (question, language) → [cited articles] judgements anyone can verify by opening the PDF. No LLM judge, and no self-generated queries scored against themselves.

Yield is itself a finding: of 546 Q&A blocks extracted from five documents, only 141 (25%) cite an article resolvable to an indexed instrument. The PRIIPs consolidated Q&A yields just 4%, because its answers cite annexes — the KID methodology lives there rather than in numbered articles, and this system indexes articles only. An abort gate refuses to proceed below 120 usable pairs. It fired at 19, survived two parser fixes to 71, and passed only at 141 after three more source documents were added.

Known gaps, stated rather than hidden

  • held_out_instrument: 0 judgements. No PRIIPs RTS citations survived extraction.
  • held_out_direction: 0 judgements. Every ESMA question is in English, so nl→en and de→fr never occur. The spec promised holdouts the data cannot populate.
  • 64 distinct articles are cited across 141 questions, so effective diversity is 64, not 141.
  • Single seed. Only large gaps are claimed.
Provenance

Completeness is a defect, not a metric.

Every result carries the verbatim passage, instrument, article number, CELEX id, language, version indexed, retrieval date, and a EUR-Lex deep link anchored to the article. Anything below 100% fails the build rather than lowering a score.

MiFID II has been amended repeatedly. Consolidated text differs from the original, and citing the wrong version is a compliance error — so the version is recorded and displayed with every hit.

The EUR-Lex web UI sits behind a bot challenge that returns 202 with an empty body to automated clients. The corpus is fetched instead through the Publications Office Cellar endpoint, the sanctioned machine-to-machine route.
